## The Fundamental Problem

People make shitty choices.

At the polls. In their lives. In their relationships. In their work.

Not because they're stupid. Not because they lack values. But because they can't project the consequences.

It's too complex. Too many time horizons. Too many dimensions. Too many perspectives to integrate.

So they react to last night's news segment. To the emotion of the moment. To the tweet that pissed them off this morning.

And the next day, when the consequences arrive, they're shocked. "How is this possible?" Because you didn't project. You reacted.

---

## The Vision

**The ultimate citizen is the one who knows why they say what they say.**

Not the one with the right opinions. Not the one who votes for the right side. The one who understands the consequences of their choices, judged by their own values, and who decides with full awareness.

You never say "I'm for" or "I'm against" without:
1. A deep understanding of the consequences
2. A clear awareness of how YOU judge them
3. The ability to adjust your criteria if you realize they don't match what you truly want

## The Three Components

### 1. The Fundamental Truths

There are roughly a dozen things humanity mostly agrees on.

| Truth | Universal preference |
|-------|---------------------|
| **Health** | Time alive in good health > time alive sick |
| **Resources** | Access to goods and services > deprivation |
| **Safety** | Feeling physically safe > feeling in danger |
| **Relationships** | Meaningful connections > isolation |
| **Autonomy** | Ability to choose > helplessness |
| **Recognition** | Being seen and valued > being ignored |
| **Purpose** | Life with direction > life without meaning |
| **Growth** | Development > stagnation |

These truths aren't political opinions. They're deep biological and psychological preferences. Almost nobody prefers being sick, isolated, and helpless.

### 2. The Personal Grid

But these truths conflict with each other. And that's where personality emerges.

**Freedom vs Equality** — the fundamental left-right divide.
- Some prefer to maximize freedom, even if it creates inequality
- Others prefer to maximize equality, even if it limits freedom
- Nobody is 100% on one side

**Security vs Adventure**
- Some optimize for stability
- Others optimize for exploration
- The dial shifts depending on the area of life

**Individual vs Collective**
- Some prioritize personal development
- Others prioritize contributing to the group
- Both are legitimate at different times

**How do you discover your grid?**

Not by asking. People lie — not on purpose, but because they don't know.

By creating choice conflicts. Dilemmas where you must sacrifice one value for another. From these conflicts emerges your true weighting.

## The Link to Economics

Sébastien made a crucial observation: our economic model solves the freedom-equality dilemma.

**The classic problem:**
- The right wants freedom (no taxes, no redistribution)
- The left wants equality (income taxes, redistribution)
- The usual compromise satisfies nobody

**Our solution — Tax immobility, not movement:**

| Actor | Return | Storage Tax 1%/year | Effective tax |
|-------|--------|---------------------|---------------|
| Passive rentier | 1.5% | 1% | **66%** of their gains |
| Active entrepreneur | 20% | 1% | **5%** of their gains |

Same rate. Radically different incentives.

- **Freedom preserved** — you can do whatever you want with your money
- **Equality promoted** — passive accumulation is discouraged, resources circulate

This is a solution that does both. Not a compromise. A reconciliation.

And when the enlightened citizen sees this policy:
1. The engine projects the consequences (who wins, who loses, at what horizon)
2. Their grid evaluates (according to MY values, is this positive?)
3. They can adjust their dials if they realize their declared values don't match their reaction

The system doesn't tell you what to think. It shows you what you actually think.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                             │
│   1. YOU DECLARE your values (first approximation)                          │
│                                                                             │
│   2. YOU DECIDE when faced with concrete choices                            │
│                                                                             │
│   3. THE SYSTEM OBSERVES the gap between declaration and decision           │
│                                                                             │
│   4. THE SYSTEM CALIBRATES your real grid (what you do > what you say)      │
│                                                                             │
│   5. YOU SEE THE GAP — "you say you value X but you choose Y"              │
│                                                                             │
│   6. YOU CAN ADJUST — either your declarations, or your behaviors           │
│                                                                             │
│   → Back to 2, continuous loop                                              │
│                                                                             │
```

## Why Now?

The tools finally exist.

**Causal projection** — LLMs can model complex consequences
**Cognitive calibration** — Behavioral data allows discovery of true preferences
**Programmable economics** — Smart contracts allow encoding equitable rules

But the window is short.

If the decision-support systems that emerge are optimized for engagement (Big Tech), they'll amplify biases instead of correcting them. They'll show you what makes you click, not what makes you think.

We're building the alternative. A system that helps you become the citizen you'd want to be — not by telling you what to think, but by showing you what you actually think and what your choices imply.

## PRIMARY OBJECTIVES (ranked)

1. **Content confidentiality** — Non-public Moments, Narratives, and Things are unreadable without the correct decryption keys. A database dump yields ciphertext, not plaintext. This is the non-negotiable core: private content stays private, even against infrastructure-level attackers.

2. **Key distribution via graph** — No external key server, no separate PKI infrastructure, no out-of-band key exchange. Space keys live on HAS_ACCESS links between Actors and Spaces — the same links that encode access roles. The graph is its own key distribution system.

3. **Hierarchical access** — A single HAS_ACCESS link to a parent Space cascades access to all child Spaces connected via IN links. One key to a guild hall unlocks every room inside it. This prevents link explosion (no need for Actor-to-every-child-Space links) and mirrors real-world spatial access patterns.

4. **Zero-knowledge database** — If FalkorDB is compromised, the attacker sees full topology (nodes, links, energy, weights) but zero content. They know Actor A talks to Actor B in Space S. They cannot read what was said. The shape of the world is visible; the substance is opaque.

5. **Seamless UX** — Encryption must be invisible to users during normal operation. Humans use a wallet-like mechanism (Chrome extension or Mind app) that stores private keys and handles decrypt transparently. AIs use `.keys/` directories mounted as volumes — generated by mind-mcp per citizen per repo, available at instance launch. No one types a passphrase to read a Moment.

## NON-OBJECTIVES

- **We do NOT encrypt topology.** Link structure, node existence, energy values, weight values, and node types remain in plaintext. The physics engine requires these for propagation, decay, and moment flips. Encrypting topology would kill the physics — and physics is the foundation of everything.
- **We do NOT implement end-to-end encryption for real-time voice.** Live voice streams between visitors and citizens use transport-level encryption (TLS/DTLS). Space Encryption protects data at rest in the graph, not data in transit over WebRTC or WebSocket.
- **We do NOT build a PKI certificate authority.** There is no central certificate issuer. Each Actor generates their own key pair sovereignly. Trust between Actors is established through the graph's existing trust mechanics (trust scores, HAS_ACCESS links), not through certificate chains.
- **We do NOT encrypt public Spaces.** Piazzas, public buildings, published Narratives, and open guild announcements remain in plaintext. Encryption is applied per-Space based on its visibility setting. Public content must be indexable, searchable, and servable without key negotiation.
- **We do NOT provide plausible deniability.** The existence of private Spaces and the identity of Actors with HAS_ACCESS links are visible in the topology. An attacker knows private content exists and who can access it — they just cannot read it.

## TRADEOFFS (canonical decisions)

- When **performance** conflicts with **confidentiality**, choose **confidentiality**. Context assembly will be slower (decrypt step adds latency). Accept this. A fast system that leaks private content is worse than a slower system that protects it.
- We accept **slower context assembly** (AES-256 decrypt per Space per conversation) to preserve **privacy at rest**. The decrypt overhead for a citizen's context (~10-50 Moments) is measured in low milliseconds — acceptable for an LLM call that takes seconds.
- We accept **visible topology** to enable **graph physics**. The physics engine needs energy, weight, and link structure in plaintext to propagate, decay, and detect moment flips. Encrypting these would require decryption at every tick for every node — destroying performance and defeating the purpose of physics-as-governance.
- We accept **key management complexity** (wallet for humans, mounted volumes for AIs) to preserve **sovereign key storage**. Centralizing private keys would simplify operations but create a catastrophic single point of failure. Sovereignty means each Actor holds their own keys.
- When **revocation speed** conflicts with **cascade simplicity**, choose **cascade simplicity**. Revoking access means deleting the HAS_ACCESS link (which removes the encrypted Space key for that Actor). Child Space access is revoked implicitly via cascade. Re-encryption of the Space key for remaining Actors happens asynchronously.

## SUCCESS SIGNALS (observable)

- **Database dump test:** Exfiltrate the full FalkorDB database. Grep for known plaintext strings from private Spaces. Zero matches.
- **Key isolation test:** No Actor private key appears anywhere in the graph database. Private keys exist only in sovereign storage (wallets, `.keys/` volumes).
- **Decrypt latency:** Context assembly for a citizen conversation (decrypt Space key + decrypt 10-50 Moments) completes in < 50ms added latency.
- **Access cascade test:** Grant HAS_ACCESS to a parent Space. Verify content in all child Spaces (via IN links) is decryptable without additional link creation.
- **Revocation test:** Delete a HAS_ACCESS link. Verify the revoked Actor can no longer decrypt any content in that Space or its children.
- **Public Space test:** Public Spaces remain fully readable without any key negotiation. No encryption overhead on public content paths.
- **AI citizen boot test:** An AI citizen instance launches with `.keys/` mounted, connects to graph, and decrypts its private mind Space within 2 seconds of startup.
